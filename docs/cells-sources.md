# Cells source ledger

The horn-relay starter remains workshop-composed/inferred material. The new
worlds encode published onset/relative-pitch data. None reproduces a recording,
measured tuning, instrument sound, performance microtiming or measured duration.

## Amadinda — Buganda, Uganda

- **Ndyegulira ekkadde:** Gerd Grupe, “Notating African Music: Issues and
  Concepts,” *The World of Music* 47(2), 2005, printed pp.94–95, figure 10.
  [Institutional publication PDF](https://phaidra.kug.ac.at/detail/o:69192.pdf).
  24 pulses. The stored B cycle starts 4–2–5; this preserves the published
  relative phase, unlike starting the compact 5–4–2 cell at pulse 1.
- **Ssematimba ne Kikwabanga:** Gerhard Kubik, “Inherent patterns,” *L’Homme*
  171–172, 2004, printed p.253 figure 2; pp.255–257 for the high part.
  [Publisher PDF](https://journals.openedition.org/lhomme/pdf/24906).
  36 pulses. Source A/B sequences were visually checked in figure 2.

Rows are A lower/upper, B lower/upper, and two streams whose union is high C.
C retains source degrees 1/2 from A and B. Splitting C between voices is a
software allocation, not a claim of six traditional players.

Choice 1 retains each published part. Choices 2/3 filter lower/upper degrees:
these are **our adaptations**, not traditional variants. Ensemble recalls are
Published parts, Lower stream, Upper stream. The last two keep full C against
the filtered A/B layers. Durations (0.7 pulse), gains and 82 Hz model root are
workshop choices. The five-tone model is idealized equal pentatonic tuning;
it does not reproduce a specific amadinda's intonation or octave deviations.

## Mbira — Chakwi, Virginia Mukwesha

Grupe (2005), printed pp.98–99, figures 13–15, same institutional PDF. The
48-pulse kushaura tablature has four playing areas. We split alternating bass
and right-index attacks into two voices each, preserving their union. All 64
unparenthesized attacks remain; no accompanying kutsinhira is invented.
Choices 2/3 loop the first/second 24-pulse excerpts: workshop adaptations.

Raw manual/key tokens remain in the data. The staff mapping was visually
checked: LL3=F, LL7=D one register higher, LU3=F below LU2=G, RT2=C, RI4=E.
This is Grupe's relative notation, not measured tuning. The seven-tone model
is deliberately idealized; tritave is an explicit retuning. Gains and
0.9-pulse durations are assigned here, not read from the tablature.

## Crossing tuning worlds

Melodic events retain source-relative pitch steps separately from onset time.
Retuning rescales ordinal degrees into 13 equal divisions of a tritave, five or
seven equal divisions of an octave, or octave-folded/sorted pitch classes from
the existing beating/source-estimate maps. Rounding can merge degrees when
mapping into a smaller pitch set. Register duplication follows the selected
period, so octave pairs become tritave pairs in tritave tuning. These are
explicitly transformed interpretations. The original horn tuning maps are
unchanged for original horn events.

Changing Rhythm world keeps tuning, pulse rate and mute states, selects the new
world's first cell in every row and preserves the moving shared phase. Matched
starting points are pentatonic-model for amadinda and heptatonic-model for the
mbira study; choose tritave for an intentional non-octave transformation.
